What your receiver draws on the TV

The on-screen display, from your seat

The volume number that flashes up mid-film, the banner when the source changes, the now-playing screen that stays up over music - all of it lives in the receiver's setup menu, which means getting up and putting the menu on the screen you were watching. AVR Maestro puts it on your phone instead.

What you can change

  • Volume: Bottom, Top or Off. Where the volume readout appears when you change it, or whether it appears at all. Off is the one people go looking for during a film.
  • Info: the status banner the receiver draws when the source or the sound mode changes.
  • Now Playing: whether the now-playing screen stays up while a network source is playing, or clears itself after a moment.
  • 21:9 Mode: repositions the receiver's own menus for a 21:9 constant-height screen, so a projector does not cut them off.
  • Screen Saver: moves the on-screen image after a period of stillness, which protects a panel prone to burn-in.
Only what your receiver has

Your chassis decides which of these you see

Not every receiver has all five. Rather than showing a control that would fail, AVR Maestro asks the chassis which on-screen options it publishes and renders only those - so an older receiver with no Screen Saver simply does not get that card.

If a setting exists but the receiver did not answer this time, the app says exactly that and offers to try again. It never presents "we could not read this" as "your receiver does not have it" - those are different answers and only one of them is true.

Common questions

On-screen display, answered

Can I stop the volume number appearing on my TV?
Yes. Open Settings then On-Screen Display in AVR Maestro and set Volume to Off. You can also move it between the bottom and the top of the screen without leaving your seat.
What is 21:9 mode for?
It repositions the receiver's on-screen menus for a 21:9 constant-height screen, so a projector does not cut them off at the top and bottom. Turn it on only if you have that kind of setup.
Why do I see fewer options than the screenshots?
Because your receiver reports fewer. AVR Maestro asks the chassis which on-screen options it has and shows only those. An older receiver with no Screen Saver simply does not get that card, rather than being shown a control that would fail.
