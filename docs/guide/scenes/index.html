The headline feature

Scenes: your whole room, one tap

Movie Night, Late Night, Vinyl Evening. Each one is a single tap that sets the input, the sound mode, Audyssey or Dirac, the levels and the zones exactly the way you like them. You do not need to know your receiver's menus to build one: the app asks a few plain questions and only offers what your receiver can actually do.

★★★★★ 4.9 · 198+ ratings
Two ways to start

Tap Add Scene, then pick how you want to build it

  • Guided Scene answers a few questions with you and offers only what your receiver supports. Best for your first Scene, or when you know what you want but not which menu holds it.
  • Create from Scratch captures what your receiver is doing right now. Get it sounding right, tick the settings to keep, and save.
Nothing on the Scenes tab yet? It suggests a few starting points that fit your receiver, such as Movies and TV or Late night. Tap one and the guided steps open with it already chosen.
Guided Scene, step by step

Your first Scene in about a minute

A guided Scene is built from your answers. It does not read the receiver and saving it changes nothing, so you can try ideas freely. A step counter at the top shows where you are, and Back takes you to any earlier answer.

  1. Name it. Something you will recognise on a busy evening: Movie Night, Saturday Records, Kids' Cartoons.
  2. Say what it is for. Movies and TV, Music, Gaming, Late night, Other rooms, or Custom when you would rather choose everything yourself. The choice only highlights the settings that usually fit. Nothing is ticked for you.
  1. Tick what it should control. Settings are grouped the way you think about them: Routing & Volume, Sound, Audyssey & Dirac, Speakers & Levels, Zones, Other and Appearance. Only settings your receiver supports are listed, and the ones marked Suggested are the usual picks for what you chose in step 2.
Audyssey and Dirac Live are two different room-correction systems, and your receiver uses one per speaker preset. When Dirac Live is active some Audyssey settings are locked by the receiver itself, and the app tells you so as you tick them instead of letting you save a Scene that cannot apply. See why a control is locked.
  1. Choose a value for each setting. One short page per ticked setting, listing the values your receiver reports: its sound modes, its inputs, its Dirac slots and speaker presets, with levels shown the way the receiver shows them.
  • Show other genres. A receiver only lists the sound modes of the genre it is playing. Tap this and the app reads the others for a moment, then puts your receiver back exactly as it was.
  • Show which presets are Audyssey or Dirac. The same trick for speaker presets, so each slot is labelled before you pick one.
  • Channel levels are kept exactly as they are when you save. Want them different? Adjust them on the Speakers tab first, then come back.
  1. Review and save. The last page lists exactly what the Scene will store, grouped like the rest of the app. Tap Save and it lands in your Scenes list, ready whenever you are.
The number of steps grows with what you tick: each ticked setting gets its own page, so a three-setting Scene is shorter than a ten-setting one.
Create from Scratch

Already sounds perfect? Keep it

When the room already sounds right, there is nothing to answer. Tap Add Scene, pick Create from Scratch, give it a name and tick what to keep. The Scene stores the values your receiver has right now, and only those settings.

Picking Custom in a guided Scene gives you the same full list without reading the receiver, which is handy when you are setting up for later.

What a Scene can hold

Almost everything, and only what you pick

Applying a Scene changes only the settings it holds. A late-night Scene can turn down the volume and switch on Dynamic Volume while leaving your input, sound mode and calibration exactly where they are.

🎥

Power, routing & volume

Main-zone power, input source and master volume. A Scene can switch the receiver on as it loads.

🎵

Sound

Sound mode, Restorer, Dialog Enhancer, tone, Cinema EQ, Graphic EQ, IMAX Enhanced, speaker virtualizer and center spread.

🎲

Audyssey & Dirac

MultEQ, Dynamic EQ, Dynamic Volume, LFC, and the active Dirac Live slot.

🔊

Speakers & levels

Speaker preset plus per-channel and subwoofer levels.

🏠

Zones

Zone 2 and Zone 3 power, input and volume, and all-zone stereo.

📻

Extras

Eco, sleep timer, dimmer, HDMI audio out, the tuner station, and even the app's own color theme.

Exactly which of these appear depends on your receiver. The app lists only what your model reports, so you never build a Scene around a setting it does not have.

Using and changing a Scene

Tap to apply, hold for everything else

Tap a Scene and its tile shows Applying... while the settings go across, one at a time in a safe order. Long-press it for the rest:

  • Update with Current Settings keeps the name, icon and list of settings, and takes your receiver's values right now. No deleting and re-saving.
  • Edit Captured Settings adds or removes settings from the Scene.
  • Rename Scene and Change Icon & Color, including your own images (see Custom Icons).
  • Duplicate Scene to make a variation, say Movie Night and Movie Night (Kids Asleep), without starting over.
  • Home Automation (Pro) sends the Scene to Home Assistant or Hubitat, so a single automation can dim the lights and set the receiver together. See Home Automation.
  • Delete Scene removes it here, and the next Home Assistant sync removes the script it created.
Drag a Scene by its handle to reorder the list. Scenes also show up as cards on the Home screen, and on Android you can add the AVR Scenes widget from your launcher's widget list to fire one without opening the app.
Scenes and Smart Select / Quick Select

Your receiver's slots, under their real name

Your receiver also has a few preset slots of its own. Marantz calls them Smart Select and Denon calls them Quick Select, and AVR Maestro uses your receiver's own name on every screen: the Home screen row, the Remote tab, Customize Tabs and the setup guide. A Marantz CINEMA 30 has five of them.

  • Smart Select / Quick Select lives in the receiver. Fire a slot from the Home screen or the Remote, and long-press it to rename it.
  • Scenes live in the app: up to 20 per receiver, each with its own name, icon and color, holding exactly the settings you choose, and they can become widgets, shortcuts or Home Assistant scripts.

The two never overwrite each other, so use both: the slots for the receiver's own quick switch, Scenes for everything else.

Troubleshooting

When a Scene does not fully apply

A Scene is not one command. It is a sequence, sent in a deliberate order and paced so the receiver can keep up, which is why it takes a moment rather than happening instantly.

  • Power goes first, and a cold receiver gets time to wake. If the Scene turns the receiver on, everything else waits for it to come up, because a receiver still booting ignores settings.
  • Channel levels are checked and re-sent once. Receivers drop the odd channel when a lot arrives at once. The app reads back what landed, re-sends what did not, and tells you which channels it had to retry.
  • A locked control cannot be applied, by anything. If your speaker preset is owned by Dirac Live, the receiver itself refuses Audyssey changes, and a Scene carrying them is refused the same way the on-screen control is. See why a control is locked.
  • Only what the Scene holds is changed. If a Scene is not changing something you expected, it was probably not ticked. Long-press it and use Edit Captured Settings rather than making a second Scene.
  • Scenes belong to one receiver. The list is the list for the receiver currently selected. Another receiver has its own.
Common questions

Scenes FAQ

What is a Scene?
A Scene is one tap that sets your receiver up for something: a movie, a record, a game, a quiet late night. It can hold the input, volume, sound mode, Audyssey or Dirac, speaker preset, channel levels, zones, the tuner station and power. When you apply it, only the settings you chose are changed, and everything else is left exactly as it is.
How do I make my first Scene?
Open the Scenes tab and tap Add Scene, then pick Guided Scene. Give it a name, say what it is for (Movies and TV, Music, Gaming, Late night, Other rooms or Custom), tick what it should control, choose a value for each setting from the ones your receiver offers, and check the summary before you save. It takes about a minute.
Does saving a guided Scene change anything on my receiver?
No. A guided Scene is built from the choices you make, so nothing is read from the receiver and nothing is sent to it when you save. It only changes the receiver when you apply the Scene.
What is Create from Scratch for?
For capturing a setup you already have. Get the receiver sounding the way you want, tap Add Scene, pick Create from Scratch, name it and tick the settings to keep. The Scene stores the values your receiver has right now.
Will applying a Scene overload my receiver?
No. Settings are sent one at a time in a safe, paced order, power first, and the tile shows Applying... until the receiver has taken them. Channel levels are read back and anything the receiver dropped is sent once more.
Can I change a Scene later?
Yes. Long-press it for Apply Scene, Update with Current Settings, Edit Captured Settings, Rename Scene, Change Icon & Color, Duplicate Scene, Home Automation (Pro) and Delete Scene. Drag a Scene by its handle to reorder the list.
Can a Scene turn my receiver on?
Yes. Include the power state and applying the Scene switches the main zone on (or off) first, as long as the receiver has network standby enabled so the app can reach it while it sleeps.
What is the difference between Scenes and Smart Select or Quick Select?
Smart Select (Marantz) and Quick Select (Denon) are the same receiver feature under two names: a handful of slots stored inside the receiver, five on a Marantz CINEMA 30. AVR Maestro calls them by your receiver's own name everywhere in the app, lets you fire them from the Home screen and the Remote, and lets you rename them. Scenes are AVR Maestro's own: up to 20 per receiver, each with a name, icon and color, holding exactly the settings you pick, and they can become home screen widgets and shortcuts or be sent to Home Assistant or Hubitat. The two never overwrite each other.
