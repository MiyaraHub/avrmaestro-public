Plain-language definitions

The AVR Maestro glossary

Short, plain explanations of the terms used in AVR Maestro and in this guide. Which of these your receiver has depends on its model.

The app

  • Scene. A saved combination of receiver settings, such as input, sound mode and volume, that you apply with one tap. See Scenes.
  • Quick Select / Smart Select. The receiver's own memory slots for input, volume and sound mode. Denon calls them Quick Select, Marantz calls them Smart Select. See Remote.
  • Volume preset. One of 8 saved volume levels in the app. Tap to jump to it. See Volume.
  • Demo Mode. Runs a simulated Marantz CINEMA 30 inside the app, so you can try every screen without a receiver. Nothing is sent over your network.
  • Home Automation Pro. An add-on that connects your receiver to Home Assistant, Hubitat and webhooks. See Home Automation.
  • Assistant. Ask for receiver changes in plain words. It runs on your device and only appears on devices that can run it. See Assistant.
  • Webhook. A web address that another system calls to make the app do something, or that the app calls when something happens.
  • Home Assistant / HACS / Hubitat. Home automation systems. HACS is the Home Assistant Community Store, used to install add-ons. See Home Assistant dashboard.
  • Locked control. A setting the receiver will not change right now, usually because of the sound mode or signal. See Locked Controls.

Network and connection

  • IP address. The receiver's address on your home network, for example 192.168.1.20. The app uses it to connect.
  • MAC address. A fixed hardware ID of the receiver's network port. Shown in Device Information.
  • HTTP and Telnet. The two ways the app talks to the receiver over your network. HTTP sends commands, Telnet carries live updates back.
  • HEOS. The streaming and multi-room system built into newer Denon and Marantz receivers. See Now Playing.
  • Network Standby. A receiver setting that keeps its network on in standby, so the app can turn it on.

Calibration and room correction

  • Audyssey MultEQ. Denon and Marantz room correction. It measures your room with a microphone and corrects the sound. MultEQ XT32 is the most detailed version.
  • Dynamic EQ. Audyssey feature that keeps bass and surround balanced at low volume. While it is on, Tone Control is locked.
  • Reference Level Offset. Tells Dynamic EQ how loud your content was mixed, to tune its correction.
  • Dynamic Volume. Audyssey feature that evens out sudden loud and quiet passages.
  • LFC (Low Frequency Containment). Audyssey feature that reduces bass reaching neighbouring rooms. Containment Amount sets how much.
  • Dirac Live. Room correction from Dirac, available on some receivers. When active it replaces Audyssey. Its results are stored in slots.
  • Speaker Preset. Preset 1 or Preset 2: two complete speaker setups, each with its own layout, distances, levels, crossovers and calibration.

Sound modes and processing

  • Dolby Atmos / DTS:X. Surround formats that include height channels.
  • Dolby Surround. Dolby's upmixer, which spreads stereo or surround across all your speakers. Center Spread works only in this mode.
  • Neural:X. The DTS upmixer. Available with DTS:X sound modes.
  • Auro-3D / Auro-Matic. Immersive format and upmixer from Auro Technologies, on receivers that support it.
  • IMAX Enhanced. Settings for IMAX Enhanced content, on receivers that support it.
  • Pure Direct / Direct. Sound modes that bypass most processing for the cleanest signal path.
  • DRC (Dynamic Range Compression). Reduces the gap between loud and quiet sounds. Works with Dolby signals.
  • Loudness Management. Keeps Dolby content at a consistent level.
  • Dialog Enhancer. Makes voices easier to hear.
  • Cinema EQ. Softens harsh treble in film soundtracks.
  • M-DAX. Marantz feature that restores detail to compressed music. Off, Low, Med or High.
  • Restorer. Denon feature that restores detail to compressed music.
  • Speaker Virtualizer. Simulates speakers you do not have.
  • Room Size, Panorama, Dimension, Center Width. Adjustments to how the sound field is spread, found under Sound › Shape.
  • Tone Control. Bass and treble adjustments. See Sound.
  • Graphic EQ. Per-band equalizer for each speaker. See Graphic EQ.

Speakers and bass

  • Amp Assign. How the receiver's amplifier channels are used, for example for height speakers or a second zone.
  • Crossover. The frequency below which a speaker's bass is sent to the subwoofer instead.
  • HPF / LPF. High-pass filter and low-pass filter. They split sound between speakers and subwoofer.
  • Bass management. How the receiver routes bass between speakers and subwoofers.
  • LFE. Low Frequency Effects: the separate bass channel in a film soundtrack, the .1 in 5.1.
  • Subwoofer Mode. LFE sends only the bass channel to the subwoofer. LFE + Main also sends bass from the main speakers.
  • Channel Levels. The volume of each speaker relative to the others.
  • Distance. How far each speaker is from your seat. The receiver uses it to time the sound.
  • Center Level Adjust. A quick boost or cut for the center speaker.
  • Speaker Grid. The app's live picture of your speakers and which are playing. See Speakers.

Receiver and system

  • Zone 2 / Zone 3. Extra rooms the receiver can play to, each with its own power, volume and input. See Multi-Zone.
  • All Zone Stereo. Plays the main zone's source in every zone.
  • HDMI CEC. Lets the TV and receiver control each other over HDMI.
  • eARC. Enhanced Audio Return Channel. Sends full-quality sound from the TV to the receiver over HDMI.
  • ECO Mode. Receiver power saving.
  • Auto Standby. Turns the receiver off after a period with no signal or use.
  • Sleep. A timer that turns the receiver off.
  • Trigger Out. A 12V output that switches other equipment, such as a screen or amplifier, on and off with the receiver. Has its own Remote sub-tab on receivers that have it.
  • Digital input format. Forces how the receiver reads a digital input: Auto, PCM or DTS.
  • Bluetooth Transmitter. Sends the receiver's sound to Bluetooth headphones or speakers.
  • Setup Lock / Remote Lock. Receiver locks that block menu changes or the remote control. See Locked Controls.
  • On-Screen Display. Information the receiver shows on your TV, such as the volume. See On-Screen Display.
  • Signal Info. The incoming and outgoing audio and video format, such as colour space and pixel depth.
FAQ

Terms, answered

Is Quick Select the same as Smart Select?
Yes. Denon calls the receiver's memory slots Quick Select and Marantz calls them Smart Select. AVR Maestro uses the name that matches your receiver.
What is the difference between LFE and a subwoofer?
LFE is the separate bass channel in a soundtrack. The subwoofer is the speaker that plays it, plus any bass the receiver redirects from your other speakers.
Do I need Audyssey and Dirac Live?
No. They are two kinds of room correction. On receivers with Dirac Live, an active Dirac Live calibration replaces Audyssey.
