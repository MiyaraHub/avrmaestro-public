The Sound tab

Every audio control your receiver has, in one Sound tab

The Sound tab has up to ten pages, and it mirrors your receiver's own audio menu rather than a simplified version of it. This page walks through each one: what every control does, what it expects, and why the receiver sometimes refuses to let you touch it.

What is in here

The ten pages

Open the Sound tab at the bottom of the screen. The pages run across the top; tap a page name or swipe left and right to move between them.

Your receiver decides which of these appear. A chassis without Auro-3D has no Auro-3D page, one without an IMAX Enhanced decoder has no IMAX page, and the Shape page only appears while Dolby Surround, DTS Neural:X or an Auro mode is playing. You can also hide pages you never use: Settings › Customize Tabs, then switch off the ones you do not want. That choice applies to every receiver.
Page one

Surround: pick a sound mode and switch audio features

The top card shows the current mode exactly as the receiver reports it. Below it the modes are grouped into Movie, Music, Game and Pure, and only the modes the receiver offers for the signal it is decoding right now are listed.

  1. Tap a category: Movie, Music, Game or Pure.
  2. Tap a mode, for example Dolby Surround or DTS Neural:X. The tile lights up and the current mode card changes.
  3. If a tile looks active but nothing happens, the audio does not match the mode: a Dolby option needs Dolby content and a DTS option needs DTS.

Audio Features

Under the mode list, the AUDIO FEATURES card holds switches you can change. Each row shows Active or Inactive; tap the pill to turn the feature on or off. A row that reads N/A cannot be used with the current mode or signal: tap its question mark and the app tells you what it needs.

  • Neural:X - shown in modes that can use it; it needs a DTS:X surround mode.
  • IMAX Enhanced - on receivers with IMAX. The on pill reads Auto while IMAX is armed and Active once the receiver detects an IMAX-encoded stream.
  • Cinema EQ - not available in Direct, Pure Direct, Stereo and Original Sound modes.
  • Center Spread - only with the Dolby Surround sound mode.
  • Loudness Management - needs a Dolby Digital, Dolby Digital Plus, Dolby TrueHD or Dolby Atmos signal.
  • Speaker Virtualizer - on receivers that report it; needs a Dolby Atmos, Dolby Surround or "+Dolby Surround" mode.
  • LFE Level - shown only when the receiver offers it in the current sound mode. Use minus and plus to step through the range the receiver reports. When the receiver shows LFE Level but does not allow a change right now, the row says why instead of showing a number.
  • Subwoofer - switches the subwoofer output on or off, on receivers with a subwoofer set up that allow it.

Subwoofer Level Adjust

At the bottom of the page, the SUBWOOFER LEVEL card (or SUB 1 LEVEL, SUB 2 LEVEL and so on with more than one subwoofer) is a playback offset the receiver keeps per source. It sits on top of your calibrated subwoofer level and does not change it. Tap minus or plus to step it; the step is 0.5 dB or 1 dB depending on the receiver. The card is hidden while the current source has no audio signal.

Page two

Dialog & DRC: clearer voices and night listening

  • Dialog Enhancer - Off, Low, Medium or High. Makes voices easier to follow without touching your calibration.
  • Center Level Adjust - on older receivers (the X4500H and SR7015 class) this takes the place of Dialog Enhancer. It is an absolute center speaker level that applies to every source and Quick Preset, from -12.0 to +12.0 dB in 0.5 dB steps. Tap minus or plus to change it, or the middle button to return to 0 dB. Newer receivers such as the Cinema 30 do not have it.
  • Dynamic Range Compression - Off, Low, Mid, High or Auto. Narrows the gap between whispers and explosions, which is what you want late at night.

Compression data is carried inside a Dolby bitstream, so on a PCM, DTS or network stereo source there is nothing to compress. Rather than let you press a button that does nothing, the app greys the row and says why: play Dolby Digital, Dolby Digital Plus, Dolby TrueHD or Dolby Atmos content to use it.

Page three

Audyssey: the whole calibration menu, not just MultEQ

  • MultEQ - Reference, Bypass LR, Flat or Off. Reference is the calibrated house curve; Flat removes the high-frequency roll-off; Bypass LR leaves the front pair uncorrected; Off turns room correction off, and the controls below it dim.
  • Dynamic EQ - On or Off. Keeps the tonal balance right as you turn the volume down, instead of the bass thinning out.
  • Reference Level Offset - 0, 5, 10 or 15 dB. Tells Dynamic EQ how far below reference level your content was mastered. 0 dB suits film; the higher offsets suit music and TV.
  • Dynamic Volume - Off, Light, Medium or Heavy. Evens out loud adverts against quiet dialogue.
  • Surround Level Compensation - Off, Light, Medium or Heavy.
  • Audyssey LFC - On or Off. Low Frequency Containment keeps deep bass from carrying into neighbouring rooms.
  • Containment Amount - minus and plus set how strongly LFC works. It only responds while Audyssey LFC is on; until then the page says "Enable Audyssey LFC above to adjust containment."

The round refresh button at the top right (Re-query calibration state) asks the receiver again which calibration the current Speaker Preset uses. Use it if the page still shows a banner after you switched presets on the receiver itself.

Instead of the controls you may see a banner: No Audyssey calibration on this Speaker Preset, Audyssey not active on this Speaker Preset, or Dirac owns this Speaker Preset - Audyssey unavailable. Each one is the receiver's own state. See why a control is locked.
Page four

Dirac Live: your filters, by the names you gave them

If you have run Dirac Live, the filters you exported sit in numbered slots on the receiver. AVR Maestro lists them by the name you saved them under, with the slot number underneath, plus an Off entry to run the preset with no filter at all. One tap swaps filters.

On a Speaker Preset that Audyssey owns, the page says Audyssey owns this Speaker Preset - Dirac unavailable instead of showing an empty list. The refresh button (Re-query Dirac slot info) reads the slots again.

Page five

Graphic EQ: nine bands, per speaker

A real editor: the On/Off switch at the top of the page, beside Reset and Copy, turns Graphic EQ on or off, and nine bands from 63Hz to 16kHz, -20 to +6 dB, are dragged on the graph. Choose All, Left/Right or Each speaker, use Copy to start from your measured Audyssey curve, Reset to flatten, and the pending-changes bar to apply.

The receiver runs room correction or the manual EQ, never both, so this page is locked whenever MultEQ or Dirac is running, and it names which one.

Page six

Channels: trim any speaker while the film is running

Per-channel trim in dB for every speaker present in the current signal: Front Left, Front Right, Center, the surrounds, the surround backs, the subwoofers and the height layer, each with its short name underneath so there is no guessing which is which.

  • Drag the slider for a big move, or tap plus and minus for a single fine step, both while you listen.
  • Reset All puts every channel back to 0 dB.
  • Multi-Subwoofer Output - with more than one subwoofer set up, a card tells you how many there are; trim each one (SW, SW2 and so on) in the channel list.
  • Bass Sync - the subwoofer delay trim, 0 to 16 ms. Tap minus or plus to line the subwoofer up in time with the main speakers. Shown on receivers that have it.
  • Tactile Transducer - on receivers whose own menu shows it: an Enabled switch, Level from -12 to +12 dB in 0.5 dB steps, and a Low-Pass Filter from 40 to 250 Hz.

Channel levels appear when multi-channel audio is playing. While a Dirac Live filter is active the receiver locks them, and the page says so. These are the live per-signal trims; the per-speaker reference levels stored on the Speaker Preset are on the Speakers tab instead.

Page seven

Tone: bass, treble, the DAC filter and M-DAX

  • Tone Control - the master switch. Bass and Treble only reach the receiver while this is on.
  • Bass and Treble - -6 dB to +6 dB in 1 dB steps.
  • DAC Filter - Filter 1 is warmer with a slow roll-off, Filter 2 is a sharper reference response.
  • Audio Restorer (M-DAX) - Off, Low, Med or High. Rebuilds detail lost to compressed or streaming audio, which is what you want on a low-bitrate stream and not what you want on a lossless disc.
Only Dynamic EQ disables tone control. While it is on, the page shows TONE CONTROLS LOCKED BY DYNAMIC EQ. To use Bass and Treble, turn Dynamic EQ off on the Audyssey page, or set MultEQ to Off, which also turns Dynamic EQ off.
Page eight

Shape: tune the Dolby Surround and Neural:X upmixers

These controls shape how the upmixer spreads sound around the room. They apply when Dolby Surround or DTS Neural:X is active, so the Shape page only appears while one of those modes (or an Auro mode) is playing. Switch to one on the Surround page first.

  • Room Size - Small, Med-S, Medium, Med-L or Large.
  • Panorama - On or Off. Widens the front soundstage by blending the main speakers across the center. Only audible in Dolby Surround mode.
  • Dimension - minus and plus shift the sound field towards the front or the back (Dolby Surround).
  • Center Width - minus and plus spread center-channel vocals towards the left and right main speakers.
Only the rows your receiver supports are shown. Dimension and Center Width are sent as steps, and the receiver does not report their position back, so the number on screen counts your steps from when you opened the page. Watch the receiver's on-screen display if you need its exact value.
Page nine

IMAX: the full Enhanced settings menu

On a chassis with IMAX Enhanced, the app exposes the whole menu rather than just the on/off:

  • IMAX Mode - Auto or Off.
  • Audio Settings - Auto follows the IMAX recommendation; Manual opens the controls below.
  • High Pass Filter (HPF) - 40Hz through 250Hz.
  • Low Pass Filter (LPF) - up to 250Hz; the lowest step depends on the receiver model.
  • Subwoofer - On or Off, and Subwoofer Output - LFE+Main or LFE.
Page ten

Auro-3D: upmixing with a shape

  • Auromatic 3D Preset - Small, Medium, Large, Speech or Movie. Picks the room and content character the upmixer aims at.
  • Strength - how much height information Auromatic generates. Minus and plus step through the range the receiver reports, which is printed under the value.
  • Auro 3D Mode - Direct, or Channel Expansion.

These only apply while an Auro mode is selected on the Surround page, and the page is only present on a chassis that carries the Auro-3D decoder.

Common questions

Sound tab FAQ

Why does the list of sound modes change?
Because the receiver's list changes. The modes a Denon or Marantz will accept depend on what it is decoding: a two-channel stream offers the upmixers, an Atmos stream offers the Atmos-capable modes. AVR Maestro reads the current list off the receiver each time rather than showing a fixed menu that would be wrong half the time.
Why is a control greyed out with N/A?
N/A means the receiver cannot use that feature in the current state, usually because the incoming signal or the sound mode does not carry it. Tap the question mark next to the row and the app explains what it needs. A grey control is never a bug being hidden; it is the receiver's own answer.
Why can't I see the Shape page?
The Shape page only appears while Dolby Surround, DTS Neural:X or an Auro mode is playing, and only on receivers that have at least one of its controls. Pick one of those modes on the Surround page and the Shape page appears. Also check that you have not hidden it under Settings › Customize Tabs.
What is the difference between Channels and Levels?
Channels (Sound tab) is the live per-channel trim for the signal playing right now. Levels (Speakers tab) is the per-speaker reference level stored on the Speaker Preset by your calibration. Adjusting Channels does not rewrite your calibration.
Can I use Tone Control and Audyssey together?
Not while Dynamic EQ is on, because the receiver takes over the tone controls. Turn Dynamic EQ off on the Audyssey page, or set MultEQ to Off, and Bass and Treble become editable again.
Do I need to be on the same network?
Yes. AVR Maestro talks to your receiver directly over your LAN, with no cloud account and no relay server in between. See Getting started.
